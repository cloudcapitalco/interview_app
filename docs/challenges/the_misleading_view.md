# Challenge: The misleading view

> A short, anytime probe. Pick it up whenever a screen you built could mislead a
> customer who trusted it.

## Scenario

This whole product is a bet that an honest interface wins. But honesty isn't the
absence of lying — it's the absence of *misleading*, and data-rich screens mislead by
accident all the time: a chart that hides the waste behind the savings, an average
that buries a bimodal split, a y-axis that doesn't start at zero, a "savings" number
that quietly means gross, a breakdown whose "other" bucket is doing all the work, a
partial current month compared against full ones as if they were the same.

## The task

Find a place in what you've built where a customer who *trusted the screen* would walk
away believing something false — and fix it. Then say what the honest version costs:
sometimes the truthful presentation is busier, slower, or less impressive, and the
judgment is whether that price is worth paying here.

## Deliverables

1. **The misleading view** — what it showed, and what a trusting customer would wrongly
   conclude.
2. **The fix** — the honest version, and what it gave up (visual simplicity, a bigger
   number, a faster load) to be honest.
3. **One sentence** on the general trap it's an instance of, so it's a lesson and not
   just a patch.

## What we're looking for

The reflex to audit your own interface the way a skeptical customer would, and the
integrity to fix a screen that flatters the product at the customer's expense — even
when no one would have caught it.
