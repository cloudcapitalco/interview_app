# The understanding ledger

Submit a short **understanding ledger** alongside your work (one for the whole
submission is fine — your call). It is an honest accounting of *where your
understanding is, and isn't.*

For the work you're handing in, briefly record:

- **What you verified yourself** — the parts of the result you designed, checked, or
  could reconstruct from first principles, and *how* you checked them. For a build,
  this includes the architecture you chose and why, the boundaries you drew, and the
  behavior you actually observed (not assumed) by using what you shipped.
- **What you delegated and trusted** — the parts you let an agent produce and did
  not fully verify, and *why* trusting the agent there was a reasonable call.
- **What you're unsure of** — the assumptions, edge cases, and limits you're aware
  of, and where the result is most likely to be wrong or to strain (under load, at
  scale, on inputs you didn't test).

**Why we ask.** See [what we value](./understanding_debt.md). The ledger is *not* a
confession — delegating the right things to an agent is the skill we're hiring for.
What we're looking for is an accurate map of your own understanding, because having
an accurate map is itself evidence that you have the understanding. A candidate in
understanding debt can't write an honest ledger.

**Keep it short** — a page is plenty across the whole submission. And expect us to
**probe it**: in review we may ask you to explain, from first principles, something
you listed under "verified," or to walk us through a tradeoff you made. That's the
whole game.
